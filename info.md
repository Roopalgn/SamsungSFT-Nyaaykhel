
# Samsung Solve for Tomorrow 2026 - Information Guide
 
## 1. Overview
- **What it is:** An innovation competition by Samsung, empowering young minds to develop technology-driven solutions for real-world challenges.
- **Age Group:** 14-22 years old (as of June 30 / July 3, 2026). Indian residents only.
- **Team Size:** Individuals or teams of up to 3 members.
- **Intellectual Property:** You retain full ownership of your idea and IP. Samsung and FITT (IIT Delhi) solely assist in developing it.
- **Goal:** Apply the "Design Thinking" process to come up with innovative, impactful tech ideas.
 
## 2. Competition Themes
Participants must apply to **only one** of the following four themes:
- **AI Living for India:** Leverage AI to solve public challenges, enhance security, and drive inclusive innovation.
- **Health and Education:** Enable affordable, high-quality, tech-driven healthcare and learning for underserved communities.
- **Sport & Tech:** Leverage innovation to enhance sports performance, promote inclusion, and encourage active lifestyles.
- **Environmental Sustainability:** Harness technology to drive climate action, resource efficiency, and waste reduction.
 
## 3. Prizes & Grants
- **4 Winning Teams (1 per theme):** Share of INR 2 Crore Incubation Grant (INR 50 Lakhs each) + 6-month incubation at IIT Delhi.
- **Top 20 Teams (Finalists):** INR 1 Lakh per team for prototype enhancement + Latest Samsung Galaxy Z Flip for each member.
- **Top 40 Teams (Semi-Finalists):** INR 20,000 per team for prototype development + Samsung laptops for each member.
- **Top 100 Teams:** Certificate of Participation.
- **Community Choice Award:** INR 1 Lakh each for 2 teams from the Top 20 (based on audience votes at the finale).
- **Digital Impact Award:** INR 50,000 for 1 team from the Top 20 (based on social media engagement).
- **Additional Perks:** Masterclasses, mentorship from Samsung & industry leaders, hands-on prototyping at IIT Delhi labs, visits to Samsung R&D centres, and Investor Connect opportunities.
 
## 4. Competition Phases & Timelines
- **Phase 1: Application (May 7 - July 3, 2026)**
  - Submit your idea via the online application form.
- **Phase 2: Top 100 & Top 40 Selection (July - August 2026)**
  - **July 16:** Top 100 teams announced (25 per theme). 
  - **July 17 - Aug 7:** Mandatory online training. Teams submit a video pitch and pitch deck.

  - **Sept 9 - Sept 10:** National Pitch Event at IIT Delhi.
  - **Sept 17:** Top 20 teams announced (5 per theme).
- **Phase 4: Grand Finale (October 2026)**
  - **Oct 14 - Oct 15:** 2-day Grand Finale in Delhi-NCR. Includes Investor Connect, final pitches, and the Award Ceremony.
  - **Winners Announced:** 4 Winning Teams, Community Choice Award, and Digital Impact Award.
 
## 5. Eligibility & Rules
- Open to Indian nationals only (ages 14-22).
- Minors (under 18) and applicants with disabilities must provide parental/guardian consent via email within 7 days of application submission.
- Ideas must be original. You cannot have previously received funding/awards for the identical proposal exceeding INR 5 Lakhs.
- Mandatory attendance is required for online trainings, bootcamps, and pitch events. Failure to attend results in disqualification.
- Each individual can only participate in one team and submit one idea.
 
## 6. Evaluation Criteria
Entries are judged on:
- **Innovation and Creativity:** Originality and uniqueness compared to existing solutions.
- **Impact and Feasibility:** Potential to solve real-world challenges with current technology, resources, and laws.
- **Scalability / Reach:** Ability to expand, reach the target audience, and benefit a larger community.
- **Technical and Execution Capability:** Clarity in implementation, use of research, and application of Design Thinking.
 
## 7. The Design Thinking Process (How to build your idea)
Follow these 5 steps to create and refine your solution:
1. **Empathise:** Identify who is affected by the problem. Understand their goals, pains, behaviours, and needs.
2. **Define:** Turn the problem into an opportunity. Focus on how tech can solve the specific barrier.
3. **Ideate:** Brainstorm solutions without limitations. Use techniques like "What if?", Idea Remixing, or Network Mapping.
4. **Prototype:** Bring the idea to life using sketches, paper/cardboard models, apps, or animations to test concepts quickly.
5. **Test:** Gather user feedback on the prototype, learn what works/doesn't, and refine the solution.
 
## 8. Incubation & Legal Information for Winners
- **Company Incorporation:** To receive the INR 50 Lakhs grant, the 4 winning teams must incorporate a Private Limited Company.
- **Minor Winners:** If winners are minors, the company will be incorporated with parents/guardians as promoters/directors who will manage funds in a fiduciary capacity until the minor turns 18.
- **Fund Disbursement:** The grant is disbursed in milestone-based tranches (50% on agreement, 25% on milestone, 25% on final closure). Funds must be strictly used for project development.
- **Background Checks:** Shortlisted teams may be subject to identity, age, and public conduct verifications.

the pitch that got me into top 40

before i tell u what the pitch is these are the things i cut down on after realising what i did was too much
Pick ONE hero sport for the entire pitch — kabaddi. It's the most visually dramatic (raids, touches, escapes are clean discrete "events" a camera can detect), it has by far the largest grassroots base, and judges already have a mental model of it from Pro Kabaddi. Mention wrestling and kho-kho as "expansion sports" in one sentence on the solution slide — don't build the story around all three.
Pick ONE hero feature for the demo — auto-detecting scoring events (raid points/touches) and generating a verified match record. This alone solves the "undertrained referees miss events" + "no verifiable records" problems, which are your two strongest, most concrete pain points. Athlete performance cards and the federation integrity dashboard become "what this enables next," shown as a mockup screenshot, not a live capability you have to justify technically.
Cut "flags officiating discrepancies" from the core pitch, or soften it. This is the most technically hard claim (it implies your AI is arbitrating human judgment calls) and the most politically sensitive one (you're telling federations their referees are biased — true, but a 22-year-old team saying it in a national pitch needs to be careful about tone). Reframe it as "creates an audit trail" rather than "flags bias" — same impact, less confrontational, and much easier to actually build a POC for.
